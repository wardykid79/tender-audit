Should I bid for this tender?

The 7-point bid/no-bid check

Score each point honestly. The first three are hard gates — fail one and it's usually a no-bid no matter how attractive the contract looks. The last four are judgement calls that decide whether a "maybe" becomes a "yes."

  • 1The deadline — is there genuinely time?A quality response takes longer than people think, especially if a document set or references are required. If the deadline is days away and you're starting cold, that alone can be a no-bid. In NI under the Procurement Act 2023 you'll see a "tender notice"; in Ireland it's published on eTenders. Note the clarification-question deadline too — it's usually earlier than the submission date.
  • 2Eligibility — do you actually qualify?Check the hard requirements first: minimum annual turnover, required insurance levels (public/employer's/professional indemnity), and tax position (tax clearance in Ireland; supplier standing in NI). If you can't meet a stated minimum, you're excluded — stop here.
  • 3Documents — can you produce the full set?Public tenders ask for evidence: financial statements, references, policies (health & safety, GDPR, environmental), certifications, and a self-declaration (the ESPD in Ireland). If a single mandatory document is missing and can't be produced in time, that's a no-bid.
  • 4Scoring — does the weighting favour you?Find the award criteria (the price-vs-quality split). If 70% rides on quality questions you can't strongly evidence, your odds drop — even at a great price. Bid where the marks reward what you're genuinely good at.
  • 5Cost of bidding — what will this really take?Estimate the hours to write, gather documents, and review. A heavy bid for a small contract often isn't worth it. Your time has an opportunity cost: hours on a long-shot bid are hours not spent on work you'd actually win.
  • 6Competition — what's your real chance?Is there an incumbent who'll be hard to beat? How many bidders are likely? You don't need certainty — just an honest read on whether you're a credible contender or making up the numbers.
  • 7Profitability — is the contract worth winning?Run the numbers at the price you'd realistically bid. A contract you win but can't deliver profitably is worse than one you skip. Watch for thin margins, onerous terms, or KPIs (NI contracts now carry at least three) you can't comfortably hit.

Turning the seven points into a decision

It's simpler than it looks:

  • Bid — you clear all three hard gates, the scoring suits you, and the maths works. Commit properly.
  • Maybe — you qualify, but something's marginal (tight deadline, awkward scoring, thin margin). Bid only if you can fix the weak point or the contract is strategically worth it.
  • No-Bid — you fail a hard gate, or the cost and odds don't justify the effort. Walk away early and keep your time for a better fit. Saying no to the wrong tender is a win.
Cross-border note: Since 24 February 2025, Northern Ireland tenders follow the UK Procurement Act 2023 (new terminology, the "competitive flexible procedure," and at least three KPIs per contract). Ireland still follows its EU-based regime on eTenders. If you bid on both sides of the border, the checks are the same — but the paperwork and language differ. We cover both.
